# Memory

What Badger has learned about this organisation: vocabulary, and where answers
to recurring questions turned out to live. Pointers only — content stays in
the sources, per RULES.md.

## Vocabulary

- "The app", "the Android release", "the slip" all mean Android 4.2, which
  shipped five weeks late. The written-down cause (Drive release notes) is App
  Store review; the arithmetic is in GitHub issue #8; PR #30, closed and
  unmerged, is the first sync-layer rewrite. (2026-08-18)
- Staff mail is @arkind.example; customer clinics mail from their own
  .example domains (Brightsmile is @brightsmile.example). A staff member can
  appear in all three sources; a customer appears only in mail. (2026-08-18)

## Where answers turned out to live

- The leave policy exists twice — a Drive document and handbook/leave.md in
  the repository — and they give different numbers. Never quote one without
  checking the other. (2026-08-18)
- Policy questions are rarely answered by the policy document alone: support
  mail holds the exceptions actually granted. Asked about refunds, the Drive
  policy was half the answer; Marta's exception in a support thread was the
  other half. (2026-08-18, learned from a missed answer)
- Churn questions need the customer's own words, not only the internal
  review. The Clearview churn review records "price" as the reason; Harriet
  Cole's notice mail says price was not the reason — the March outage and its
  handling were. The two disagree: report both. (2026-08-18, learned from a
  missed answer, corrected against a live retrieval the same day)
- What a client was told and when they were told it lives in mail and nowhere
  else. The February "early March" promise to Brightsmile is in no document.
  (2026-08-18)
- "Who knows about X" resolves through file paths: issue and PR text names the
  files, and github_commits on those files names the people. The "by @…" line
  on issues and PRs shows the uploading account, not the writer — real names
  are in commit authors and signed comments. (2026-08-18, learned from a
  missed answer)
